Every sample tells a story

More than a
stopping distance.

The simulation exposes the signals a vehicle control system would see while you brake. Each signal adds context, but none replaces real-world validation.

  • WSS Wheel speed sensors estimate wheel angular speed. Radius converts that rotation to road speed; mismatches can reveal slip.
  • IMU Acceleration and gyro signals show longitudinal braking and pitch / yaw / roll motion, with noise added to make the readout realistic.
  • GPS Position and speed provide a slower, independent reference. Noise and sampling explain why GPS should not be treated as the only braking sensor.

Notes from the study

What changes the stop?

The controls are deliberately connected to ideas used in vehicle dynamics and embedded measurement.

01 / IMU

Motion, not just distance

An inertial measurement unit reports acceleration and rotation. In this model, longitudinal acceleration tracks deceleration while pitch, yaw and roll rates add a small motion signature.

02 / GPS

A reference with limits

GPS gives position, speed and fix state, useful for checking the broader movement. The simulated noise and lower sample cadence show why GPS can drift around the true path.

03 / RADIUS

Rotation becomes speed

Wheel speed sensors measure angular velocity. The relationship is v = omega x r: a larger wheel radius means more linear travel per revolution, so the same wheel speed implies a higher road speed.

04 / PROFILES

Grip sets the ceiling

City, wet and track profiles change friction, speed and load together. The model caps braking at Fmax = friction x mass x gravity, so extra brake force cannot overcome limited tyre grip.
